Organelle Quality Hub

Organelle Quality Hub is a mini-search engine that searches for information on the complete organelle genome records of eukaryotic organisms and their quality values. You can filter by species, organelle type, and even genes. Here, we give you a basic breakdown of features pertaining to organelle quality. Here you can view things like ambiguity content, RNAs reported, GC content, and a list of genes.

For chloroplasts and other plastids, you can also view if any inverted repeats were ever reported along with inverted repeats that could be inferred by BLAST.


FAQs


What exactly are organelles?


Almost all eukaryotic cells have various subunits within them required to perform basic functions; we call these organelles. Some of these organelles, such as mitochondria (responsible for cellular respiration) and chloroplasts (responsible for photosynthesis and are actually part of a much broader category of organelles called plastids), are believed to have evolved from bacteria billions of years ago and have remnants of their ancestral DNA within them. Though these genomes are much smaller than those of normal bacteria, they can still be sequenced. This is the information Organelle Quality Hub tries to provide.


What are inverted repeats?


Basically, these are two separate sections of the genome that are "opposites" (complements) of each other and, when lined up, match each other's opposing base. For example, IrA (the first inverted repeat) could look like:


GCTTATTCTCTATGCGGGG

and then IrB would be the same sequence backwards and have its A bases swapped with Ts and Cs swapped with Gs (and vice versa) and it would look like:
CCCCGCATAGAGAATAAGC
